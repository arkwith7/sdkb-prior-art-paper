## 1. Scope — what was ported and what was not

What was ported is the procedure. The four formal validation layers (L0–L3), the cross-task
condition (T3) and the fault-injection procedure were moved by replacing a profile file, with no
code change. What was not ported is the task benchmark. Conditions T1 and T2 require a ground-truth
set and a candidate pool, so they were excluded, and the acceptance rule is therefore not completed
on this resource. The `accept` field of the verdict artifact is `null` in every row, and only the
conjunction of the formal layers and the cross-task layer is recorded, as `Accept_partial`.
**A partial acceptance rule is not called an acceptance rule.**

One statement is permitted: that adjudication was carried out on a second domain by the same
procedure, confined to the formal layers and the cross-task layer. The preregistration §0 forbade
six statements before results were seen, and that list is enforced mechanically by the `EP5` entry
of `paper/verdicts.yaml`.

## 2. Frozen list

Frozen before execution: the per-file sha256 of the resources (six T-Box releases, two development
A-Boxes, one holdout A-Box); the full text and sha256 of the 15 competency questions; three delta
shape rules; the specification and random seed of the three cross-task fault families; the
generation rule for the 30 synthetic sound deltas; the definitions of migration conditions R and N;
the decision rule; the threshold grid; the promotion rule for design principles; and the forbidden
wordings. The complete list is in §1–§7 of the preregistration document.

The `expect-min` of every competency question was fixed at 1, and that value was set before
execution. Two questions that returned 0 rows on the development set were removed before
adjudication and two replacements were written; every removal and replacement took place on the
development set. A competency question returning 0 rows on the holdout is not removed and is
reported as unmet.

## 3. Two rules for reading the results

First, **the adjudicable denominator is 12, not 21.** The nine containment-inversion faults found 0
injection sites in the holdout model and therefore never entered the graph. Counting an instance
that was never injected as a detection failure would record the gate as having missed something it
never had the chance to miss.

Second, **detection by T3 alone of 0 is not carried over into a failure of the gate.** The
development model expresses the building–floor–room containment with `brick:hasPart`, which yielded
90 injection sites and detected faults, whereas the holdout model expresses the same relation with
`brick:isPartOf`. What was observed is that a fault specification does not transfer unchanged
across resources; porting a procedure and porting a fault specification are different problems. The
remedy is deficit ⑨ of Table 10 in the manuscript.
