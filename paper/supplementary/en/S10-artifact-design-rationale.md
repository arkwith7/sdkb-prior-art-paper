## 1. Fixed specification of the scoring function — the values come from the code

**Why this table exists.** The term definitions and the final weights of the ranking function lived
only in prose, and as a result §5.6 of [S5](S5-submission-full-v2.md) described `ConceptOverlap` as
a *"weighted Jaccard"* across several revisions. The code computes the intersection over the union
of two `frozenset` objects, that is, an **unweighted Jaccard**. As long as the values are copied by
hand the same drift returns, so the table is generated from the code.

**Because `w_h = 0`, a resource change that alters only the hierarchy or its alignment is in principle invisible to the scores of P0★ and P1.** This value is the outcome of a grid selection on the development set and is not a preregistered item.

**`ConceptOverlap` is unweighted.** No per-concept and no document-frequency weighting enters any configuration, and enlarging the vocabulary of the resource enlarges the denominator with it — the reading of §5.2 of the results rests on this fact.

## 2. What this specification imposes on the reading of the results

**First, the hierarchy term did not operate.** Because `w_h = 0`, a resource change that alters only
the hierarchy or its alignment is in principle invisible to the scores of the two proposed
configurations. The failure to meet the non-inferiority criterion observed in the controlled
resource replacement (EP3) has to be read together with this fact.

**Second, the concept term is unweighted.** Enlarging the vocabulary of the resource enlarges the
denominator of the Jaccard as well, so an increase in the number of concepts per document does not
by itself mean an improvement in the score. The observation that the resource metric and the
retrieval performance moved in opposite directions is consistent with this structure.

**Third, the candidate pool is not enlarged.** The two proposed configurations only rerank the top
1,000 of the text hybrid, so no weighting recovers a document outside that pool. Candidate
generation is outside the design of this study, and the same boundary is recorded in the
competing-explanation table of [S5](S5-submission-full-v2.md) §7.9.

## 4. Alternatives in the design of the artifact, and the choice made (moved from §3.1 of the manuscript)

**The alternative of three separate ontologies.** The three tasks could be held as three independent
ontologies joined by alignment mappings. Under that arrangement each task could evolve its schema
independently. The mappings would then have to be maintained as assets of their own, and the effect
of a change in one ontology on another task would be hidden behind them. Because the purpose of this
study is to observe that effect before release, we chose a shared core. The price is coupling: the
shared vocabulary becomes a propagation path for regression, which is why the cross-task condition
T3 is required in the acceptance rule.

**Defining view boundaries by query path.** The second decision was to define the boundary of a view
by its query path rather than by an exclusive partition of classes. On the semiconductor floor the
same equipment, process, and material vocabulary is used by equipment engineering, patent practice,
and technology planning, and it does not divide along organizational lines. Duplicating classes per
view would give the same object different identifiers and would remove the link between tasks
altogether. Instead the dataset shares identifiers, separates views by query, and identifies which
view has been damaged through that view's competency-question suite.

**Why the extension contract requires four assets.** Separating a competency-question suite per view
is what makes it possible to identify, after a change, which view lost function. A concrete
propagation is the following. Merging two concepts to raise recall increases the candidates of the
prior-art view while degrading the discrimination of the expert-matching view. Structural checking
and logical consistency checking observe only the interior of the changed view and do not see this
propagation, and that is the ground for placing the cross-task condition T3 in the acceptance rule.
